The META4.0 program at ENISE bridges materials science with mechanical engineering, focusing specifically on advanced machining operations and surface metrology.

01Project overview

Precision finish turning with empirical process modeling.

Industrial machining requires strict parameter control to guarantee surface integrity, productivity and prevent tool failure. This project utilized TMP methodology to define the optimal working domain for the finish turning process, systematically evaluating cutting speeds and feed rates to develop mathematical models capable of predicting cutting parameters for safe, full-scale production.

±5%

Process Reliability

Experimental specific cutting pressures and surface roughness must fall within a strict ±5% deviation to guarantee mechanical stability and safe chip formation.

≥

Conservative Modeling

Python optimization algorithms applied massive numerical penalties to under-predictions, ensuring calculated loads and roughness always exceeded lab measurements.

03Process mapping & modeling approach

Secure the stable machining envelope before predicting output performance.

Baseline

Establish a proven starting point by testing theoretical & manufacturer recommendations against lab produced results.

Define

Systematically increase cutting speed and feed to the allowable limits, identified via chip formation or surface finish failure.

Isolate

Map the competing constraints of acceptable chip breaking and surface roughness to isolate the optimal "working domain" for the machine.

Safeguard

Develop predictive mathematical models with built-in conservative margins, ensuring industrial settings never exceed the calculated predictions.

Safety principleSafe production cannot rely on a single benchmark. The true operational domain is defined by the strict intersection of all limitations, requiring the adoption of the most conservative minimum and maximum thresholds to prevent both tool failure and surface degradation simultaneously.
